To receive the old-age pension, you must have been enrolled in the National Pension for at least 10 years, allowing you to receive a stable monthly pension once you reach the eligible age. It is common to see people approaching retirement worry about how much pension they will receive, as the amount varies significantly depending on the length of enrollment and income level. Some wonder if simply completing 10 years is enough, while others worry about whether continuing to work will reduce their pension. In this article, we will examine in detail the basic conditions for the old-age pension, how benefits are calculated, and its relationship with the basic pension. We will explain the pension system, which can seem complex, in an easy-to-understand manner using specific examples.
